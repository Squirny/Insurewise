Car insurance in Indonesia 2026: what every EV owner needs to know

By the Insurewise team · 8 June 2026 · 8 min read

Indonesia's electric vehicle boom is real. EVs accounted for roughly 15% of new car sales in Q1 2026 — nearly double the share from a year earlier — with BYD leading the segment at 48% market share. But Indonesia's insurance market has not fully caught up. Standard motor insurance policies, written under decades-old AAUI industry clauses, routinely exclude battery damage and have limited repair networks for the brands now filling Jakarta's roads. This article explains how car insurance works in Indonesia, what EV owners face differently, and what businesses running company vehicles or fleets need to watch out for.

How car insurance works in Indonesia

Motor vehicle insurance in Indonesia is regulated by OJK (Otoritas Jasa Keuangan), which sets minimum and maximum premium rates by vehicle value range and region. There are two core coverage types:

All Risk (Comprehensive)

Covers damage from accidents, vandalism, natural disasters, fire and theft — including minor dents and scratches. Best for newer, higher-value vehicles. Premiums are a percentage of vehicle value, typically 1.5–3.5% per year depending on the OJK zone and vehicle price band.

TLO (Total Loss Only)

Only pays out if the vehicle is stolen or damaged beyond 75% of its value. Much cheaper — premiums of 0.2–0.69% per year. Practical for older or lower-value vehicles, or as a cost-control option for large fleets where individual repair claims are self-funded.

OJK divides Indonesia into three zones for pricing purposes: Zone 1 (Sumatra), Zone 2 (Jakarta and surrounding areas), and Zone 3 (the rest of Indonesia). Zone 2 typically carries the highest premiums due to traffic density and theft rates.

The EV gap: what standard policies do not cover

This is the critical issue for EV owners in Indonesia right now. The AAUI (Asosiasi Asuransi Umum Indonesia) standard motor insurance clause was written for conventional internal combustion vehicles. Under these clauses, battery damage is typically classified as an exclusion — meaning if your battery is damaged in a flood, by overcharging, through degradation, or in an accident, a standard policy may not pay.

⚠️ Battery replacement in Indonesia can cost Rp 150–400 million depending on the EV brand and model — roughly 30–60% of the vehicle's purchase price. Without specific battery cover, this is an uninsured exposure that most EV owners do not realise they carry.

The leading insurers — including Astra Insurance, Adira, and Allianz Indonesia — have responded by creating special EV riders that add battery coverage. The key things to check when buying EV insurance:

  • Battery damage rider: Explicitly covers battery repair or replacement due to accidents, flooding, or electrical faults. Confirm whether degradation is included — many riders only cover sudden damage, not gradual capacity loss.
  • Authorised service centres: Check that your insurer's partner workshop network includes an authorised service centre for your specific EV brand. BYD, Wuling, and Hyundai have expanding networks in Java, but coverage outside major cities can be thin.
  • Flood cover: Jakarta flooding is a real risk. An EV's battery and electrical systems are particularly vulnerable. Confirm flood damage is included — some basic policies exclude it.
  • Roadside assistance: Running out of charge mid-journey is not the same as running out of petrol. Confirm your policy includes 24/7 roadside assistance with EV-specific recovery (flatbed, not tow) and access to charging.

The 2026 cost squeeze: rupiah, parts, and repair inflation

EVs already cost more to insure than equivalent conventional cars in Indonesia, for two structural reasons: higher vehicle values (especially Chinese brands with well-equipped base models) and the expense of specialist repairs. The weak rupiah makes this worse in 2026.

With the rupiah at a record low of around Rp 18,020 per USD — down roughly 7.5% since January — the cost of imported EV components, battery modules, and electrical parts has risen sharply in rupiah terms. Repair costs feed directly into insurer loss ratios, which are then reflected in higher premiums at renewal. For EV fleet operators in particular, this means budgeting for 10–20% premium increases in H2 2026 is prudent.

One partial offset: BYD and Wuling are both investing in local production in Indonesia under the government's TKDN incentive scheme. Higher local content over time will reduce the FX exposure on parts — but this will take several years to materially affect repair cost structures.

What businesses with company vehicles should do

If your company operates vehicles — whether a single car used by a director or a fleet of delivery or sales vehicles — the insurance considerations go beyond personal motor cover. Key actions for 2026:

  • Audit your fleet policy for EV gaps. If you have added EVs to a fleet policy originally written for conventional vehicles, check explicitly whether battery damage is covered. Many fleets discovered this gap only at claims time.
  • Consider fleet-level cover. A company with five or more vehicles typically qualifies for a fleet policy, which pools the vehicles under one premium calculation and is often more cost-efficient than individual policies per vehicle.
  • Confirm liability cover for employees driving company vehicles. Standard motor insurance covers physical damage. Employer liability for accidents while an employee is on company business is a separate question — and one your commercial liability policy should address.
  • Lock in renewal rates before mid-year if possible. Given rupiah pressure and rising EV repair costs in 2026, the longer you wait to renew, the higher the quoted rate may be.

Frequently asked questions

Is my EV battery covered by standard Indonesian car insurance?+

Usually not under standard AAUI clauses. You need a specific battery damage rider. Ask your insurer or broker to confirm in writing that battery damage — including from flooding and electrical faults — is explicitly covered before you buy.

Does the weak rupiah affect my car insurance premium?+

Yes, especially for EVs. Most EV parts and battery components are imported and priced in USD or CNY. When the rupiah weakens, repair costs rise in local terms. Insurers factor this into renewal pricing, so expect upward pressure on premiums in H2 2026.

Should I choose All Risk or TLO for my EV?+

All Risk is strongly recommended for new EVs. The high vehicle value and expensive battery make partial-damage scenarios (flood, collision) very costly to self-fund. TLO is only practical for older EVs worth under Rp 150 million where the premium saving outweighs the repair risk.

Which insurers offer good EV coverage in Indonesia?+

Astra Insurance, Adira Insurance, Allianz Indonesia, MSIG, and Oona Insurance have all developed EV-specific products or riders as of 2026. Coverage terms vary significantly — particularly around battery cover, workshop networks, and roadside assistance — so comparing policies properly matters.

This article is general information, not insurance advice. Motor insurance terms, OJK tariff regulations and EV product availability change regularly. Always read the policy wording carefully and confirm coverage with your insurer or broker before purchasing. Rupiah exchange rate figures are as of early June 2026.
