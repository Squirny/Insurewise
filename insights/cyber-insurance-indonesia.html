Cyber insurance in Indonesia: why UU PDP makes it essential

By the Insurewise team · 3 June 2026 · 7 min read

Indonesia is one of Southeast Asia's most attacked digital economies. Government and industry trackers have recorded billions of attempted cyber-attacks and tens of millions of exposed records in recent years, hitting everyone from ministries to e-commerce platforms to small businesses. At the same time, the Personal Data Protection Law (UU PDP) now carries real penalties for companies that mishandle customer data. Cyber insurance has moved from a "big-corporate" luxury to a practical safeguard for any Indonesian business that holds customer data or depends on systems to operate. Here is what it is, why it matters now, and how to think about cover.

Why now: UU PDP and a rising threat

Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 27 of 2022) took full effect after its transition period ended in October 2024. Businesses that act as data controllers or processors must now protect personal data and can face administrative sanctions — including fines of up to 2% of annual revenue — as well as orders to suspend processing or delete data, with criminal provisions for serious misuse. A data breach is no longer just an IT problem; it is a legal and financial one.

The shift in one line: under UU PDP, failing to protect customer data can cost you a regulatory fine, legal claims and reputational damage — exactly the costs cyber insurance is built to absorb.

What is cyber insurance?

Cyber insurance protects your business against the financial fallout of a cyber incident — a hack, ransomware attack, data breach or system failure. Good policies cover both your own losses (first-party) and claims others make against you (third-party), and crucially include hands-on incident response when something goes wrong.

First-party cover (your losses)

  • Incident & breach response — forensics, IT recovery, legal and notification costs
  • Business interruption — lost income while systems are down
  • Cyber extortion / ransomware — ransom handling and recovery
  • Data restoration — rebuilding lost or corrupted data
  • Cyber fraud / funds transfer — losses from social-engineering scams

Third-party cover (claims against you)

  • Privacy & data liability — claims from customers whose data leaked
  • Regulatory defence — costs of UU PDP investigations and penalties (where insurable)
  • Network security liability — passing malware to others
  • Media liability — defamation or IP issues from your content

Who needs it most in Indonesia

  • E-commerce, retail & F&B — large volumes of customer and payment data
  • Financial services, fintech & cooperatives — prime ransomware and fraud targets
  • Healthcare & clinics — sensitive patient records under strict protection
  • Professional services & startups — client data and reliance on cloud systems
  • Any SME that takes online payments or stores customer contact data

What it costs and the 2026 underwriting reality

Cyber premiums depend on your revenue, the type and volume of data you hold, your sector and — increasingly — your security controls. In today's market, insurers expect a baseline of cyber hygiene before they will offer cover or a competitive price. Common 2026 requirements include:

  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA) on email, remote access and admin accounts
  • Endpoint detection & response (EDR) and regular, tested backups
  • Staff security-awareness training and a basic incident plan
  • Note: many policies now carry exclusions for certain AI-related and nation-state risks — read the wording

Because pricing and terms swing widely by insurer and security posture, a broker is genuinely valuable here: we match your profile to the right market and help you meet the controls that unlock better premiums.

The global picture (briefly)

Cyber is one of the fastest-growing insurance lines in the world — global premiums are measured in the tens of billions of dollars and forecast to keep growing at double-digit rates for the next decade. Indonesia's own cybersecurity spending is rising just as fast as PDP enforcement pushes companies to act. The direction of travel is clear: cyber cover is becoming standard, not optional.

Frequently asked questions

Is cyber insurance required by law in Indonesia?+

No — UU PDP requires you to protect personal data, but it does not mandate insurance. Cyber cover is a way to manage the financial risk if you suffer a breach despite your safeguards.

We're a small business — do we really need it?+

Attackers often target SMEs precisely because defences are weaker. If you take online payments or store customer data, a single breach can be existential — and cyber cover plus incident response is what helps you survive it.

Does it cover ransomware payments?+

Many policies include cyber-extortion cover, subject to terms and legal limits. Just as important is the response service that helps you contain the attack and recover data.
