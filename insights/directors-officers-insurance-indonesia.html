Directors & Officers

The director's target: why your personal assets are the new collateral in 2026

By the Insurewise team · 5 June 2026 · 8 min read

For decades, many Indonesian SME directors felt shielded by their corporate entities. But as we move through 2026, that shield is thinning. Between the new Coretax mandate, stricter OJK transparency standards, and a government-led crackdown on licensing, the liability for corporate failures is increasingly stopping at the director's front door.

If you believe a "limited liability" structure fully protects your family's savings, you may be underestimating the current Indonesian regulatory environment. At Insurewise, we are seeing a sharp increase in demand for Directors & Officers (D&O) insurance — not as a luxury, but as a practical necessity for directors who want to separate their personal exposure from their company's risk.

Coretax: automated scrutiny of every corporate filing

Beginning with the 2026 filing cycle, Indonesia's Directorate General of Taxes mandated that all corporate taxpayers submit their annual income tax returns through Coretax — the government's new integrated tax administration platform. By mid-2026, over 18 million taxpayer accounts had been activated on the system.

Coretax is more than a software migration. It integrates e-Faktur invoice data, e-Bupot withholding records and payroll submissions into a single auditable ledger. Discrepancies between what a company reports and what its counterparties have declared are surfaced automatically. For directors, this means inconsistencies in wage or benefit reporting — even unintentional ones — are far more likely to be detected and flagged.

The liability chain: under Indonesia's Manpower Law (most recently amended by the Job Creation Law), violations of wage and benefit obligations can attract fines of up to IDR 400 million, or criminal sanctions. If the company cannot satisfy those obligations, regulators and creditors may look to establish personal director liability — particularly where negligent oversight by a director contributed to the breach.

The benefit mismanagement landmine

With the 2024 launch of the Coordination Between Insurance Providers (KAPJ) framework and the 2025 KRIS hospital class standardisation, employee benefits have become a fiduciary minefield. Directors are legally responsible for ensuring their workforce is registered with BPJS and that contributions are remitted accurately and on time.

A fiduciary claim arises when an employee alleges they suffered a quantifiable loss because a director mismanaged their benefits. If an employee is denied a covered hospital room because the company failed to properly structure its KAPJ top-up policy, that employee — or their family — can sue the director personally for the difference. D&O insurance covers the legal defence and settlement costs for these claims; without it, they come out of the director's own pocket.

The enforcement reality: Koster and the end of impunity

In July 2025, Bali Governor I Wayan Koster personally oversaw the demolition of 48 illegal business structures at Bingin Beach, involving more than 500 enforcement officers. The businesses — warungs, guesthouses and restaurants — had operated for years without proper permits on state-owned coastal land.

The message to Indonesian business directors was unambiguous: length of operation does not create legitimacy. Physical, visible enforcement is back — and it is reaching businesses of all sizes. For SME directors, a licensing or zoning dispute can now trigger shareholder claims for negligent oversight, with personal liability attached if the company cannot absorb the resulting asset losses.

D&O as a talent retention tool

Director's liability is not only a personal financial concern — it is becoming a recruitment issue. Experienced senior executives in Jakarta and Bali are increasingly scrutinising the governance frameworks of companies before joining their boards. A company that cannot demonstrate adequate D&O coverage signals poor risk management at the highest level.

Providing D&O coverage is, in practical terms, a statement that your company takes its governance obligations seriously — and that it will not ask its directors to absorb personal financial risk on behalf of the business.

What a D&O policy actually covers

A Directors & Officers policy provides three core types of protection:

Side A — Personal indemnification

Covers the director directly when the company is unable or unwilling to indemnify them — the most critical protection when a company is insolvent or in regulatory receivership.

Side B — Corporate reimbursement

Reimburses the company when it has advanced funds on behalf of a director to cover defence costs and settlements.

Side C — Entity coverage

Covers the company itself when it is named as a co-defendant in securities or regulatory claims.

Frequently asked questions

How does D&O insurance protect against Coretax audit penalties?+

D&O covers directors against negligent oversight claims triggered by Coretax audit findings. It pays legal defence fees during government investigations, provides financial backing for settlements where personal liability is established, and absorbs costs even when the director is ultimately cleared. It does not cover intentional fraud or deliberate illegal acts.

Can a director be personally sued for benefit mismanagement?+

Yes. Under Indonesia's fiduciary liability framework, employees or their families can pursue personal claims against directors who failed to properly manage benefit registrations or contributions. If a KAPJ top-up wasn't correctly structured and an employee was denied a covered hospital room, the director can be held personally liable for the financial gap. D&O insurance covers the cost of defending and settling these claims.

Does D&O insurance cover criminal liability?+

No. D&O covers civil claims, legal defence costs and settlements arising from negligence, errors of judgement and alleged wrongful acts. It does not cover intentional fraud, deliberate illegal acts or criminal conduct. Think of it as protection against honest mistakes and oversight failures, not a shield for misconduct.

Is D&O insurance available for SMEs in Indonesia?+

Yes. While D&O was historically associated with large listed companies, it is increasingly available to SMEs — particularly those with significant employee benefit obligations, OJK-regulated activities or material property and licensing exposure. Insurewise can source indicative quotes from multiple local and international insurers.

What triggers a D&O claim in practice?+

Common triggers include: a shareholder suing directors for decisions that led to financial loss; employees or regulators claiming benefits were mismanaged; a government audit or investigation that alleges negligent filing; a vendor or competitor claiming misrepresentation; or a licensing failure that results in asset seizure or business closure.

This article is general information, not legal or financial advice. Regulatory requirements and enforcement practices change frequently — confirm your specific obligations with a qualified professional, and check policy wording carefully, as D&O terms vary significantly between insurers.
