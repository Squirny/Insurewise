Liability insurance: what it is and why your business needs it

By the Insurewise team · 2 June 2026 · 7 min read

Most business owners insure their building and their stock, but forget the risk that can be just as damaging: being held legally responsible for harm to someone else. A customer slips in your store, a product you sold causes injury, or advice you gave leads to a financial loss — and suddenly you are facing legal costs and a compensation claim. Liability insurance exists precisely for these moments. Here is what it is, what it covers, and why it belongs in every business's protection plan.

What is liability insurance?

Liability insurance covers the legal costs and compensation your business may have to pay when a third party holds you responsible for injury, property damage or negligence. Instead of paying lawyers and settlements out of your own pocket, the policy responds. There are three main types, and many businesses need more than one:

  • Public liability — injury or property damage to third parties at your premises or from your operations.
  • Product liability — harm caused by a product you make, sell or distribute.
  • Professional indemnity — financial loss a client suffers from your advice, design or professional service.
  • Optional: directors' & officers' (D&O) liability for company leaders.

Liability cover pays two things: the cost of defending the claim (lawyers, experts, court) and the compensation if you are found responsible. Even a claim you ultimately win can be expensive to defend.

Why liability insurance is a good idea

A single liability claim can be large enough to threaten an otherwise healthy business. Beyond the financial protection, liability cover often opens doors: corporate clients, shopping malls, landlords and government tenders frequently require proof of public or product liability before they will sign a contract. Having cover in place lets you bid for bigger work with confidence, and lets you operate knowing an accident will not come straight out of your own funds.

Who needs it most?

  • Contractors, service providers and event organisers
  • Manufacturers, distributors and retailers (product liability)
  • Consultants, agencies and professional firms (professional indemnity)
  • Any business that hosts customers or visitors on its premises

Key takeaways

  • Liability insurance covers legal defence and compensation for third-party claims.
  • Public, product and professional liability cover different risks — many firms need a mix.
  • It is frequently required to win contracts and tenders.
  • One claim can be business-ending — cover turns that into a manageable premium.
