Property insurance: what it is, why you need it, and the Iran war effect

By the Insurewise team · 2 June 2026 · 7 min read

Your premises, machinery and stock are often the largest assets your business owns — and the hardest to replace quickly if something goes wrong. Property insurance exists to make sure a fire, flood or other disaster does not become the end of your business. In this guide we explain what property insurance covers, why every Indonesian business should have it, and how a major global event — the 2026 US-Iran war — is quietly affecting property and reinsurance pricing worldwide.

What is property insurance?

Property insurance protects the physical assets your business depends on against sudden, accidental loss or damage. The most common foundation in Indonesia is fire insurance (FLEXAS) — covering fire, lightning, explosion, aircraft impact and smoke — which can be broadened into a Property All Risks (PAR) policy that covers a wider range of perils unless they are specifically excluded.

A typical programme can be tailored to include the cover your location actually needs:

  • Fire, lightning, explosion, aircraft impact and smoke (FLEXAS)
  • Flood, storm, typhoon and water damage (TSFWD)
  • Earthquake, volcanic eruption and tsunami (EQVET)
  • Riot, strike, malicious damage and civil commotion (RSMDCC)
  • Theft and burglary, and machinery or electronic equipment
  • Business interruption — lost income while you recover

The most overlooked part is business interruption: it replaces the income and ongoing costs (like payroll and rent) you keep paying while your premises are rebuilt. For many companies, the lost income hurts more than the damaged building.

Why property insurance is a good idea

Indonesia is exposed to fire, flooding and seismic risk, and a single event can wipe out years of investment. Property insurance turns an unpredictable, potentially business-ending loss into a manageable annual premium. It lets you rebuild and replace without draining cash reserves, keeps income flowing through business interruption cover, and is often required by landlords, lenders and large clients before they will do business with you.

The Iran war effect on property insurance

Market note (June 2026): Following the US-Israel air war against Iran that began in late February 2026 and the disruption around the Strait of Hormuz, specialty insurance markets have tightened. Insurers and reinsurers face heightened uncertainty in terrorism and political-violence lines, and prolonged hostilities are pushing up underwriting caution, especially for assets seen as politically or strategically sensitive.

How does a conflict in the Middle East reach a property policy in Indonesia? Mostly through reinsurance. Local insurers pass part of their risk to global reinsurers, and when global events raise uncertainty and claims pressure, reinsurance becomes more expensive. Those costs gradually filter down into property premiums and into the terms insurers are willing to offer — for example, stricter wording on terrorism, war and political-violence exclusions. Energy and commodity price swings linked to the conflict can also raise rebuilding costs, which means the sum you insure for today may need reviewing.

The practical takeaway for Indonesian businesses: lock in cover now rather than later, make sure your sum insured reflects today's rebuilding costs (not the price you paid years ago), and check exactly how war, terrorism and political-violence are treated in your wording. An independent broker can compare insurers and find the broadest cover before conditions tighten further.

Key takeaways

  • Property insurance covers your building, contents, machinery, stock — and lost income.
  • Business interruption cover is often the most valuable part.
  • Global events like the 2026 Iran war raise reinsurance costs and tighten terms.
  • Review your sum insured and war/terrorism wording — and lock in cover early.
