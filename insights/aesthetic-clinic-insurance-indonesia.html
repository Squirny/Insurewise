Property & Liability

Aesthetic clinic insurance in Indonesia: property, liability and loss of income

By the Insurewise team · 8 June 2026 · 8 min read

Indonesia's aesthetic clinic industry is one of the fastest-growing small business sectors in the country. There were 4,438 licensed aesthetic clinics by late 2024 — a figure growing at roughly 20% per year — and the overall medical aesthetics market is forecast to expand from USD 257 million to over USD 1.4 billion by 2030. Gen Z and millennial spending on skin treatments, filler, laser, and body contouring is driving the boom. But most clinic owners are running a business with Rp 500 million to several billion rupiah in equipment, a daily revenue stream of millions, and real third-party liability exposure — without any insurance at all.

Why aesthetic clinics are underinsured

Most clinic owners focus on equipment purchases, licensing, staff training, and marketing — in that order. Insurance rarely makes the priority list. There are also few insurance products in Indonesia explicitly designed for aesthetic clinics, which means owners either buy a generic property policy that misses key risks, or buy nothing at all. The result is a sector where a single equipment failure, a patient complaint, or a fire can cause losses that close the business permanently.

The three covers every aesthetic clinic needs

🏢

1. Property All Risks

Covers the physical clinic — equipment, fit-out, furniture and building — against fire, flood, short circuit and theft.

⚖️

2. Professional Liability

Covers claims from patients alleging injury, adverse reactions or negligence from treatments — filler, laser, chemical peel, botox and more.

📉

3. Business Interruption

Replaces lost revenue and covers fixed costs — rent, salaries, loan repayments — while the clinic is forced to close after an insured event.

1. Property All Risks: protecting your equipment investment

Aesthetic clinic equipment is expensive. A medical-grade laser device costs Rp 300–800 million. An IPL machine runs Rp 100–300 million. A full clinic fit-out — treatment rooms, reception, sterilisation area, signage — typically represents Rp 500 million to Rp 2 billion in capital. All of it sits in a shophouse or mall unit exposed to electrical faults, Jakarta flooding, and fire from neighbouring premises.

A Property All Risks (PAR) policy covers sudden and accidental physical loss or damage to the clinic's contents and fit-out from virtually any cause — unless specifically excluded. For an aesthetic clinic, the critical inclusions are fire and FLEXAS cover, flood and water damage, electrical short circuit and equipment breakdown (as an add-on), earthquake and volcanic eruption given Indonesia's seismic profile, and theft following forced entry.

Sum insured tip: insure for full replacement cost — what it would cost to replace the equipment and fit-out new today, not the depreciated book value. Underinsuring means partial claims are paid proportionally short.

2. Professional liability: the risk most clinic owners ignore

Every aesthetic treatment carries a risk of adverse outcomes — not because the practitioner is negligent, but because human physiology is unpredictable. A filler treatment can cause vascular occlusion. A laser session can result in hyperpigmentation. A chemical peel can scar. When outcomes disappoint or cause harm, patients increasingly seek compensation — through social media pressure, consumer protection bodies (BPKN), or formal legal channels.

Professional liability insurance — sometimes called medical professional indemnity in a clinic context — covers the legal defence costs and any compensation payments if a patient makes a claim against the clinic alleging that a treatment caused harm. Without it, defending even a minor claim can cost tens of millions of rupiah in legal fees before any settlement is reached.

⚠️ The UU PDP angle: if your clinic stores patient records, treatment photos and contact data — and most do — you are now a data controller under Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Law. A data breach exposing patient aesthetic records is both a reputational and regulatory liability. A cyber extension on your policy addresses this separately from professional liability.

3. Business interruption: protecting your income when the clinic closes

A clinic that cannot operate earns nothing — but its fixed costs do not stop. Rent on a Jakarta shophouse or mall unit runs Rp 20–80 million per month. Staff salaries continue. Equipment lease payments do not pause. Business interruption (BI) cover bridges the financial gap when an insured event — fire, flood, serious equipment damage — forces the clinic to close for repairs.

BI cover is usually purchased alongside the property policy and pays out for: lost net profit (the revenue the clinic would have earned), continuing fixed expenses that must still be paid, and the cost of operating from a temporary location if needed. The indemnity period — how long the policy pays — is typically set at 6 or 12 months. For an aesthetic clinic, 12 months is safer: major equipment replacement and full refurbishment after a fire typically takes longer than owners expect.

Getting the gross profit figure right matters. BI policies pay based on your declared gross profit — revenue minus variable costs. Declare this accurately and review it annually as your clinic grows. An underdeclared figure results in underinsurance at claim time.

Frequently asked questions

Does standard property insurance cover aesthetic equipment?+

A standard fire-only policy (PSAKI) will cover equipment damaged by fire, but not electrical breakdown, short circuit damage, or equipment malfunction. A Property All Risks policy gives much broader cover. For high-value laser and IPL devices, confirm specifically with your broker that the equipment is listed and valued correctly.

What does professional liability NOT cover for a clinic?+

Standard exclusions include intentional acts, criminal proceedings, claims arising from unlicensed procedures, and contractual guarantees of results (never guarantee a specific outcome in writing). The policy covers claims alleging negligence — not deliberate misconduct.

How much does insurance for an aesthetic clinic typically cost?+

It depends on your sum insured, location, revenue and risk profile. As a rough guide, a clinic with Rp 1 billion in equipment and fit-out in Jakarta might pay Rp 3–6 million per year for PAR cover, plus separate pricing for BI and professional liability based on annual revenue. A broker can give you an accurate figure quickly.

Do I need insurance if I rent the clinic space?" +

Yes — the building owner's insurance covers the building structure, not your equipment, fit-out or liability. As a tenant you are responsible for your own contents and any liability arising from your business operations. Most mall and shophouse landlords also require tenants to carry their own public liability cover.

This article is general information, not legal or insurance advice. Coverage terms, exclusions and pricing vary by insurer and policy. Consult a licensed broker for a quote specific to your clinic's risk profile.
