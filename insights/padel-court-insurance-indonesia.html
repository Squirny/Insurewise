Padel court insurance in Indonesia: why the boom needs cover

By the Insurewise team · 3 June 2026 · 7 min read

Padel is one of the fastest-growing businesses in Indonesia. According to Core and Court data cited by Kontan (June 2026), Indonesia's padel industry grew from just 3 clubs and 15 courts in 2021 to 293 clubs and 947 courts by end-2025 — one of the fastest expansions of any sport in the region, concentrated in Jakarta, Bandung and other big cities. With hourly rates of roughly Rp250,000–450,000 and operators reporting payback in around 1.5–2 years, the rush to build is understandable. But in the race to open, one thing is routinely left off the checklist: insurance. A padel court is a high-value building full of glass, lighting and fast-moving players — exactly the kind of asset that should never be left uninsured.

How exposed is the padel boom? Insurance is lagging the build-out

There is no official figure for how many Indonesian padel courts carry proper insurance, but the pattern across fast-growing sports venues is consistent: construction races ahead of risk management. Many new operators are first-time venue owners who focus on courts, bookings and marketing, and treat insurance as an afterthought — if at all. In more mature padel markets abroad, specialist cover is standard precisely because claims happen; Indonesia's market is younger, which means penetration is almost certainly lower and the protection gap wider.

A snapshot from our own backyard: in Kemang, South Jakarta — one of the city's padel hotspots — we found that only 4 of 13 padel clubs carried insurance. That is under a third covered, meaning roughly two in three venues run a multi-billion-rupiah, publicly-accessed asset with no protection. It is a small local sample, not a national survey, but it matches what we see across the market: the padel protection gap is wide and real.

Why a padel court should be insured

Two big exposures sit at the heart of every padel venue: the asset and the people on it.

  • A large capital asset at risk — glass walls, steel frame, artificial turf, LED lighting, the building shell and your clubhouse or cafe. Fire, flood, storm, electrical faults or riot can damage all of it at once.
  • Injuries to players and visitors — padel is fast and enclosed. Balls cause eye and facial injuries, players collide with glass walls, and wet artificial turf leads to slips and falls. Any of these can become a liability claim against the operator.
  • Lost income if you have to close — if a fire or flood shuts your courts for weeks, business interruption cover replaces the rental income you would have earned.
  • Lender and landlord requirements — if you financed the fit-out or lease the space, the bank or landlord will usually require property and liability cover.

What padel court insurance typically covers

A complete programme for a padel venue usually combines a few cover types into one package:

  • Property All Risk (PAR) — the building, glass walls, turf, nets, lighting and equipment against fire, flood, storm, theft and accidental damage (including earthquake as an add-on).
  • Public liability — injury to players, spectators or visitors, and damage to their property, while on your premises.
  • Business interruption — lost rental income while you repair after an insured event.
  • Glass cover — specific protection for the expensive tempered-glass walls that define a padel court.
  • Optional add-ons — money, employee/personal accident, and equipment breakdown for lighting and HVAC.

Premiums depend on your sum insured (the rebuild and equipment value), the location and its flood/earthquake exposure, and the liability limit you choose. Frequently asked questions

Is insurance mandatory for a padel court in Indonesia?+

It is not generally a legal requirement, but lenders, landlords and many corporate tenants require it — and operating an uninsured high-value venue with public access is a serious financial risk.

Does property insurance cover the glass walls?+

Yes — the tempered-glass walls, frame, turf and lighting are insured under a Property All Risks policy, including breakage and replacement, subject to policy terms.

What liability limit should a padel court choose?+

It depends on visitor numbers and risk appetite. Because injuries in an enclosed court can be serious, we usually recommend a meaningful public liability limit and can advise the right level for your venue.
