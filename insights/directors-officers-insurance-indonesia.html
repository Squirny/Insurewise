The Director's Target: Why Your Personal Assets Are the New Collateral in 2026

Coretax, stricter OJK standards, and a government crackdown on licensing are shifting corporate liability directly to the director's door.

Insurewise | June 2026 | 8 min read

For decades, many Indonesian SME directors felt shielded by their corporate entities. But as we move through 2026, that shield is thinning. Between the new Coretax mandate, stricter OJK transparency standards, and a government-led crackdown on licensing, the liability for corporate failures is increasingly stopping at the director's front door.

If you believe a "limited liability" structure fully protects your family's savings, you may be underestimating the current Indonesian regulatory environment. At Insurewise, we are seeing a sharp increase in demand for Directors & Officers (D&O) insurance — not as a luxury, but as a practical necessity for directors who want to separate their personal exposure from their company's risk.

1. Coretax: Automated Scrutiny of Every Corporate Filing

Beginning with the 2026 filing cycle, Indonesia's Directorate General of Taxes mandated that all corporate taxpayers submit their annual income tax returns through Coretax — the government's new integrated tax administration platform. By mid-2026, over 18 million taxpayer accounts had been activated on the system.

Coretax is more than a software migration. It integrates e-Faktur invoice data, e-Bupot withholding records, and payroll submissions into a single auditable ledger. Discrepancies between what a company reports and what its counterparties have declared are surfaced automatically. For directors, this means that inconsistencies in wage or benefit reporting — even unintentional ones — are far more likely to be detected and flagged.

The liability chain: Under Indonesia's Manpower Law (most recently amended by the Job Creation Law), violations of wage and benefit obligations can attract fines of up to IDR 400 million, or criminal sanctions. If the company cannot satisfy those obligations, regulators and creditors may look to establish personal director liability — particularly where "negligent oversight" by a director contributed to the breach.

2. The Benefit Mismanagement Landmine

With the 2024 launch of the Coordination Between Insurance Providers (KAPJ) framework and the 2025 KRIS hospital class standardization, employee benefits have become a fiduciary minefield. Directors are legally responsible for ensuring their workforce is registered with BPJS and that contributions are remitted accurately and on time.

A fiduciary claim arises when an employee alleges they suffered a quantifiable loss because a director mismanaged their benefits. If an employee is denied a covered hospital room because the company failed to properly structure its KAPJ top-up policy, that employee — or their family — can sue the director personally for the difference. D&O insurance covers the legal defence and settlement costs for these claims; without it, they are paid out of the director's own pocket.

3. The Enforcement Reality: Koster and the End of Impunity

In July 2025, Bali Governor I Wayan Koster personally oversaw the demolition of 48 illegal business structures at Bingin Beach, involving more than 500 enforcement officers. The businesses — warungs, guesthouses, and restaurants — had operated for years without proper permits on state-owned coastal land.

The message to Indonesian business directors was unambiguous: length of operation does not create legitimacy. Physical, visible enforcement is back — and it is reaching businesses of all sizes. For SME directors, a licensing or zoning dispute can now trigger shareholder claims for negligent oversight — for failing to ensure the company's operating permits were in order — with personal liability attached if the company cannot absorb the resulting asset losses.

4. D&O as a Talent Retention Tool

Director's liability is not only a personal financial concern — it is becoming a recruitment issue. Experienced senior executives in Jakarta and Bali are increasingly scrutinising the governance frameworks of companies before joining their boards. A company that cannot demonstrate adequate D&O coverage signals poor risk management at the highest level.

Providing D&O coverage is, in practical terms, a statement that your company takes its governance obligations seriously — and that it will not ask its directors to absorb personal financial risk on behalf of the business.

What a D&O Policy Actually Covers

A Directors & Officers policy provides three core types of protection:

  • Side A — Personal indemnification: Covers the director directly when the company is unable or unwilling to indemnify them. This is the most critical protection when a company is insolvent or in regulatory receivership.
  • Side B — Corporate reimbursement: Reimburses the company when it has advanced funds on behalf of a director to cover defence costs and settlements.
  • Side C — Entity coverage: Covers the company itself when it is named as a co-defendant in securities or regulatory claims.
Important limitation: D&O insurance covers negligence, errors of judgement, and wrongful acts. It does not cover deliberate fraud or intentional illegal acts. It is a defence tool, not a licence for misconduct.

How Does D&O Insurance Protect Against Coretax Audit Risks?

Protection against "negligent oversight" claims

The Coretax system is designed to detect filing discrepancies in real time. If an audit flags an error in wage or benefit reporting, shareholders or regulators may sue directors for negligent oversight that harmed the company's financial position. D&O insurance specifically covers organisation leadership against these types of wrongful act claims.

Coverage for financial penalties pushed to directors personally

Wage and benefit violations can trigger fines of up to IDR 400 million under Indonesia's Manpower Law. These are ordinarily a company liability — but where the company is unable to pay, or where a director's personal negligence is established, D&O insurance provides financial backing for the resulting settlements and costs.

Absorbing legal fees — even when the director is cleared

Defending against a government audit investigation or a related fiduciary claim generates substantial legal costs — regardless of outcome. A D&O policy covers defence fees and investigation costs from the moment a claim is made, protecting personal assets even when the director is ultimately found to have acted properly.

Can a director be personally sued for benefit mismanagement?

Yes. Under Indonesia's fiduciary liability framework, employees or their families can pursue personal claims against directors who failed to properly manage benefit registrations or contributions. If a KAPJ top-up wasn't correctly structured and an employee was denied a covered hospital room, the director can be held personally liable for the financial gap. D&O insurance covers the cost of defending and settling these claims.

Does D&O insurance cover criminal liability?

No. D&O insurance covers civil claims, legal defence costs, and settlements arising from negligence, errors of judgement, and alleged wrongful acts. It does not cover intentional fraud, deliberate illegal acts, or criminal conduct. Directors should view D&O as protection against honest mistakes and oversight failures — not as cover for misconduct.

Is D&O insurance available for SMEs in Indonesia?

Yes. While D&O was historically associated with large listed companies, the policy is increasingly available to SMEs — particularly those with significant employee benefit obligations, OJK-regulated activities, or material property and licensing exposure.
