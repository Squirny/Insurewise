Kost and boarding house insurance in Indonesia: property, loss of rent and liability

By the Insurewise team · 8 June 2026 · 7 min read

A rumah kost is one of Indonesia's most common property investments. In Jakarta alone, hundreds of thousands of kost units house students, young workers and migrants from across the archipelago. For many owners, the kost is their primary income source — monthly rent from 10, 20 or 30 rooms funding daily living expenses, mortgage repayments or retirement. Yet most kost buildings are uninsured. A single fire, a serious flood, or a structural failure can wipe out months or years of income and leave the owner with an unfinanced rebuild. This article explains the three insurance covers every kost owner should have.

🏠

1. Property All Risks

Covers the building and contents against fire, flood, earthquake, short circuit and other physical damage.

📋

2. Loss of Rent

Pays the rental income you lose while the building is being repaired and cannot be occupied — protecting your cash flow.

⚖️

3. Owner's Liability

Covers legal claims from tenants or visitors injured on the property — a broken stair, a faulty electrical installation, a collapsing wall.

1. Property All Risks: rebuilding after fire or flood

Fire is the most common total-loss event for kost buildings. Crowded urban plots, dense electrical wiring, LPG cooking and proximity to neighbouring properties all elevate the risk. Jakarta flooding damages ground-floor rooms, common areas and electrical systems every wet season. Earthquake is an ever-present background risk across Java and beyond.

A Property All Risks policy covers the building (walls, floors, roof, fixtures) and contents (furniture, appliances, CCTV, water pumps) against sudden physical damage from virtually any cause. The key covers to confirm for a kost are fire and FLEXAS (fire, lightning, explosion, aircraft impact, smoke), flood and storm damage, earthquake and volcanic eruption (especially important for Java), riot and malicious damage, and theft following forced entry of common areas.

Always insure for rebuild cost, not market value. A kost worth Rp 3 billion on the property market may cost only Rp 1.5 billion to rebuild. Insure the rebuild figure — overinsuring wastes premium, but underinsuring triggers an average clause that reduces every claim payout proportionally.

2. Loss of rent: protecting your income during repairs

This is the cover most kost owners have never heard of — and the one that matters most when disaster strikes. When a fire or serious flood forces tenants to evacuate and the building requires major repairs, the rental income stops. But the owner's costs do not: mortgage instalments, property tax (PBB), security costs and routine maintenance continue regardless.

Loss of rent cover (sometimes called rent clause or rental income insurance) pays the owner the rent they would have received during the repair period — typically for up to 12 or 24 months — as long as the damage was caused by an insured event under the property policy. It is usually added as an extension to the PAR policy at a relatively small additional premium.

📋 Example: fire in a 20-room kost in Depok

A short circuit in a tenant's room starts a fire that destroys the top floor — 8 rooms. Repairs take 6 months. At Rp 1.5 million per room per month, the owner loses Rp 72 million in rental income while still paying Rp 8 million/month on a KPR instalment.

Without loss of rent cover: the owner self-funds the income gap — Rp 72 million lost plus Rp 48 million in KPR payments made from savings or other income during the repair period.

With loss of rent cover: the policy pays Rp 72 million in lost rent. The property policy separately covers the rebuild cost. The owner's financial position is protected throughout.

3. Owner's liability: when a tenant or visitor gets hurt

As a kost owner, you have a legal duty of care to the people living in and visiting your property. If a tenant falls on a broken staircase, is injured by a faulty electrical installation, or is hurt when part of the building collapses, they may have grounds to claim compensation from you as the property owner. Owner's liability insurance covers the legal defence costs and any compensation awarded — protecting your personal assets from a claim that could otherwise run into hundreds of millions of rupiah.

This is particularly relevant for kost buildings with ageing infrastructure — common in properties built 15–25 years ago. Electrical wiring, water pipes, stair handrails and roofing all deteriorate over time, and the cost of a serious injury claim can far exceed what a landlord anticipates.

What does kost insurance actually cost?

Property insurance for a kost runs on the same benchmark rates as other residential and commercial property. For a building insured at Rp 1 billion in Jakarta (Zone 2), indicative annual premiums using benchmark PAR rates are approximately:

  • Fire / FLEXAS: ~Rp 886,000
  • Earthquake: ~Rp 1,430,000
  • Flood / storm / water: ~Rp 500,000
  • Loss of rent extension (6 months): typically 10–15% of the base property premium
  • Owner's liability (Rp 500 million limit): from Rp 1–3 million/year

For a Rp 1 billion kost in Jakarta, a complete programme covering property, earthquake, flood, 6 months' loss of rent and owner's liability typically costs Rp 4–7 million per year — less than one month's rent from a single room in most locations.

Frequently asked questions

Does my home insurance cover my kost building?+

Usually not. Standard home insurance covers owner-occupied residences. A commercial kost operation is a business activity and typically requires a separate commercial property policy. Confirm the occupancy type with your broker before assuming your home policy extends to the kost.

What if a tenant causes the fire — am I still covered?+

Yes — a PAR policy covers fire damage to the building regardless of whether the cause was a tenant's negligence. The insurer may then pursue recovery from the tenant separately. Your loss of rent cover also applies in this scenario.

Can I insure a kost I am still paying KPR on?+

Yes — in fact if you have a bank mortgage (KPR), the bank typically requires you to insure the building. Check whether the bank has arranged fire insurance on your behalf (many do) and whether it is adequate — bank-arranged policies often only cover fire, not flood, earthquake or loss of rent.

How is the loss of rent amount calculated?+

You declare the annual rental income from the property at policy inception. If a covered event forces rooms to be vacated, the insurer pays you the proportional rent for those rooms for the duration of the repair period, up to the indemnity period stated in the policy (usually 12 or 24 months).

This article is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Premium figures are indicative benchmarks based on standard PAR rates and will vary by location, construction, occupancy and insurer. Consult a licensed broker for a quote specific to your property.
