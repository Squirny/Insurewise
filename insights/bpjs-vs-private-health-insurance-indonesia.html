BPJS vs private health insurance: what's the difference?

By the Insurewise team · 8 June 2026 · 8 min read

Every Indonesian employer must enrol their staff in BPJS Kesehatan — the national health insurance. But most HR managers and business owners quickly discover that BPJS alone is not enough to attract and retain good people. Private group health insurance fills the gaps. This article explains exactly what each covers, where they differ, and why the smartest companies combine both — while also saving money doing it.

What is BPJS Kesehatan?

BPJS Kesehatan (Badan Penyelenggara Jaminan Sosial Kesehatan) is Indonesia's national health insurance program, part of the Jaminan Kesehatan Nasional (JKN) system. Participation is mandatory by law for all Indonesian workers and their immediate families. As an employer, you are required to register all employees and pay the contribution.

For 2026, the contribution rate for salaried employees (Pekerja Penerima Upah / PPU) is 5% of monthly salary — 4% paid by the employer and 1% by the employee — calculated on a salary ceiling of Rp 12,000,000 per month. The maximum monthly cost to the employer per employee is therefore Rp 480,000, regardless of actual salary.

BPJS covers spouse and up to three children at no additional premium. This makes it unusually cost-effective for families — but the coverage limits and access rules still apply to everyone.

What does BPJS actually cover?

BPJS covers a broad range of medical services — but with conditions attached. Treatment must follow a tiered referral system (sistem rujukan berjenjang): the patient first sees a GP at an assigned primary care facility (Faskes Tingkat Pertama / FKTP), and is only referred to a hospital if the GP cannot handle the condition. Walk-in to a specialist or private hospital without a referral is not covered.

  • Inpatient hospitalisation (rawat inap) at participating hospitals
  • Outpatient GP consultations at your assigned FKTP
  • Specialist referrals and procedures when referred correctly
  • Maternity and newborn care
  • Emergency treatment (no referral needed for genuine emergencies)
  • Chronic disease management programs (diabetes, hypertension, etc.)

Regarding room class: BPJS is transitioning to the KRIS (Kelas Rawat Inap Standar) single-class system, which standardises inpatient facilities across all hospitals. Under KRIS, all BPJS patients will receive the same standard room — ending the old Class I / II / III distinctions. This is positive for access but means employees no longer get a private or semi-private room through BPJS alone.

What private group health insurance adds

Private group health insurance (asuransi kesehatan kumpulan) is the top-up layer that most employers offer on top of BPJS. It removes the main friction points of BPJS — referrals, standard rooms, no outpatient flexibility — and makes the benefit feel real and usable to employees.

  • Cashless access — swipe a card at any network hospital, no upfront payment
  • No referral needed — go directly to a specialist or private hospital
  • Better room class — private or semi-private rooms based on your plan
  • Outpatient (rawat jalan) cover — clinic visits, diagnostics, prescriptions
  • Dental and optical cover (depending on plan)
  • Choice of hospital network — including premium private hospitals

The smarter approach: BPJS + private together (KAPJ)

Running BPJS and private insurance side-by-side is not just possible — it is the strategy most cost-conscious employers use. The coordination framework is called KAPJ (Koordinasi Antar Penanggung Jaminan). When an employee is admitted to hospital, BPJS pays first as the primary payer, and the private insurer covers the remainder (room upgrade, difference in charges, etc.) as the secondary payer.

Because BPJS absorbs the inpatient base cost, private insurers price KAPJ-coordinated plans lower than standalone cover. Companies that properly coordinate can reduce their private insurance premium by 15–30% compared to running private insurance alone — a significant saving at scale.

The main administrative requirement is that employees actually use the BPJS channel first for inpatient claims — which means HR needs to communicate the process clearly and the insurer needs to be set up for coordination. This is something we help our clients manage.

What this means for your business

BPJS compliance is non-negotiable — penalties for non-registration include fines and restrictions on accessing public services. But BPJS alone will not help you compete for talent against companies offering private health plans. The practical answer for almost every Indonesian SME is BPJS as the mandatory base, topped up with a private group health plan that adds cashless access, outpatient cover, and better facilities.

Frequently asked questions

Is BPJS Kesehatan mandatory for all employees?+

Yes. Under Indonesian law, all employers must register employees in BPJS Kesehatan and pay the 4% employer contribution. Failure to register or pay can result in administrative sanctions and fines.

Can I replace BPJS with private insurance?+

No — BPJS is mandatory and cannot be replaced. However, employers with private insurance that meets or exceeds BPJS benefit standards may apply for exemption (dispensasi), though this is rarely granted and involves significant administrative complexity. The standard approach is to run both.

How many employees do I need to get group health insurance?+

Most insurers require a minimum of 5–10 employees for a group health policy. Some will work with smaller groups, depending on the insurer and the plan. A broker can identify which markets will work for your headcount.

What is KAPJ and do all insurers support it?+

KAPJ (Koordinasi Antar Penanggung Jaminan) is the OJK-regulated framework for coordinating BPJS and private insurance claims. Most major group health insurers support it, but the coordination must be set up correctly in the policy — otherwise employees get confused and the saving is lost. Ask your broker to confirm KAPJ coordination is built into the plan.

What does KRIS mean for my private health plan?+

KRIS standardises BPJS inpatient room class to a single tier. This actually strengthens the case for private top-up insurance: employees who want a private or semi-private room now need private cover to get one, since BPJS will no longer offer Class I upgrade options.

This article is general information, not legal or financial advice. BPJS regulations, contribution rates, and KRIS implementation details can change — confirm current requirements with a qualified HR or legal professional. Insurance terms vary by insurer and policy.
