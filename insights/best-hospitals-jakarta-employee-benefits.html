Employee Benefits

Best hospitals in Jakarta for your employee benefits plan — and how to use them cashless

By the Insurewise team · 10 June 2026 · 9 min read

Hospitalisation in Jakarta is expensive. A single night in a private room at a top-tier hospital can cost Rp 1.5–4 million — before surgeon fees, specialist visits, labs, or pharmacy. For the average Indonesian employee, that bill lands with no warning and no savings buffer to absorb it.

A well-structured employee benefits plan changes this entirely. The right insurer, paired with the right hospital network, means your employees can walk into some of Jakarta's best facilities, receive treatment, and walk out without paying a single rupiah upfront.

This guide is written for HR managers and business owners. It covers the top hospitals in Jakarta, which insurers they accept, what cashless admission actually involves, and the one number in your policy that determines everything else.

Jakarta's top hospitals: what sets them apart

Jakarta has hundreds of hospitals. The ones below stand out on three criteria relevant to corporate health plans: accreditation, specialist depth, and insurance network breadth. All five are in-network for Indonesia's major group health insurers.

RS Pondok Indah Group

Three branches — Pondok Indah (South Jakarta), Puri Indah (West Jakarta), and Bintaro Jaya (South Tangerang) — all JCI-accredited. RSPI is the single hospital group with the broadest confirmed insurance partnership list in Jakarta: over 50 insurers including every major group health carrier. Specialist centres include the Pondok Indah Heart Centre, a dedicated IVF Centre, orthopaedic and spine clinics, and a gastrointestinal and liver centre. For companies whose employees are spread across the city, having three well-located branches in the same network is a practical advantage.

Siloam Hospitals

The largest private hospital network in Indonesia by number of facilities, with multiple Jakarta branches including Siloam Kebon Jeruk, Siloam ASRI, Siloam TB Simatupang, and Siloam Semanggi. Part of the Lippo Group. JCI-accredited at its flagship facilities. Siloam's network scale means most employees anywhere in Greater Jakarta will have a nearby branch — which matters when managing a claims programme across a large workforce. Accepts cashless from Allianz (via AdMedika), AIA, and most other major group insurers.

RS Premier Jatinegara (Ramsay Sime Darby)

The Indonesia arm of the international Ramsay Sime Darby healthcare group. JCI-accredited and was named Best Hospital of the Year in Indonesia by Global Health Asia Pacific for three consecutive years (2021, 2022, 2023). Strong track record in cardiac, oncology, and emergency services. Particularly useful for companies with employees in East and Central Jakarta. The international group affiliation also means international medical standards and protocols that experienced foreign employees will recognise.

RS Mitra Keluarga

One of the longest-running private hospital groups in Indonesia, operating since 1989. Multiple Jabodetabek locations including Kelapa Gading, Kalideres, Bekasi, Depok, and Cibubur — the widest geographic spread of any group on this list. KARS-accredited. A reliable mid-to-premium tier option; room rates are somewhat lower than RSPI and RS Premier, making it a practical choice for companies seeking broad employee coverage without concentrating spend at the very top of the market. Accepts cashless from Allianz, AIA, Prudential and others.

Mayapada Hospital

Two Jakarta-area locations — Kuningan (South Jakarta) and Lebak Bulus. Strong specialist offering in cardiology, oncology, and neurology. Frequently cited alongside RSPI and Siloam as a top-tier destination for employees seeking premium care. Accepts most major group insurers including Allianz, AXA, and AIA.

How cashless admission actually works

The process sounds simple — show your insurance card and walk in. In practice, a cashless admission involves a specific sequence of steps. HR teams that brief their employees on this sequence ahead of time see far fewer panicked calls on a Sunday night.

  • Present insurance card at the hospital's insurance desk

    Most network hospitals have a dedicated insurance desk separate from general admissions. Go here first. Bring the physical or digital insurance card, a valid ID, and the referring doctor's letter if applicable.

  • Hospital requests a Letter of Authorisation (LoA) from the insurer

    The hospital's insurance team contacts your insurer directly to request a Guarantee Letter (Surat Jaminan). This confirms the insurer will pay the hospital for covered services. For straightforward admissions, leading insurers like Allianz typically issue the LoA in under 20 minutes. Complex cases or admissions during off-hours may take longer.

  • Check in to the room that matches your policy entitlement

    The LoA will specify the room class covered. This is critical — see the next section. Requesting a room above your entitlement is possible but creates a co-payment obligation for every service line, not just the room difference.

  • Receive treatment — no upfront payment required for covered items

    During the stay, all covered services (room, doctors' fees within entitlement, labs, medicines on the formulary) are charged directly to the insurer. Non-covered items — lifestyle amenities, excluded diagnoses, items above policy limits — will be charged to the patient.

  • Sign off at discharge — and only pay the gap

    At checkout, the hospital will present a detailed billing split: what the insurer covers versus what the patient owes. Review this carefully before signing. Any disputes about what should be covered are resolved with the insurer directly — the hospital's insurance desk can assist.

The one number that controls everything: your room class limit

Most HR managers focus on the annual inpatient limit — the maximum total the insurer will pay in a year. That number matters, but there is a more consequential figure buried in the policy schedule: the daily room entitlement.

In Indonesian hospital billing, your room class is the anchor for everything else. Surgeon fees, specialist consultation rates, ICU charges, anaesthesia, and operating theatre costs are all priced relative to your room class. If an employee stays in a room above their policy entitlement — even by one tier — the hospital recalculates every service at the higher room class rate, and the employee pays the difference across the entire bill.

Example: an employee's policy covers a Rp 600,000/night room but they take a Rp 1,200,000/night room (double the entitlement). They expect to pay Rp 600,000/night extra. In practice, the surgeon's fee, specialist visits, and ICU rates are also scaled upward because they are anchored to room class — the total gap between what the insurer pays and what the employee owes can be 3–5× the room difference alone.

This is the most common source of employee dissatisfaction with corporate health plans — not the insurer, not the hospital, but an unexpected bill on discharge that the employee did not anticipate. The practical fix is simple: brief employees on their room entitlement before they need a hospital, not when they are standing at the admissions desk under stress.

Frequently asked questions

Which hospitals in Jakarta accept cashless from Allianz?+

RS Pondok Indah Group (all three branches), Siloam Hospitals, Mitra Keluarga, RS Premier Jatinegara, and Mayapada Hospital all accept cashless claims from Allianz via the AdMedika network. Allianz also has a Blue Eagle Network tier that provides enhanced benefits at 24 selected Jabodetabek hospitals. The full list is available on the Allianz Indonesia website and is updated periodically.

Does my employee's room class affect specialist fees?+

Yes — this is one of the most misunderstood aspects of health insurance in Indonesia. Your room entitlement is the anchor for everything else: surgeon fees, specialist consultation rates, ICU charges, and operating theatre costs are all scaled proportionally to your room class. An employee hospitalised in a room above their entitlement will typically pay the difference for every service line, not just the room itself. Brief your employees on their room entitlement before they are admitted.

Can employees use any hospital or only network hospitals?+

Cashless claims are only available at network (rekanan) hospitals. If an employee uses a non-network hospital, they must pay upfront and submit a reimbursement claim — processing takes days to weeks and reimbursement may be subject to caps or standard charge schedules rather than actual billed amounts. For genuine emergencies, most insurers allow any hospital with reimbursement afterwards. Always check the specific policy wording for your plan.

What is a Letter of Authorisation (LoA) and why does it matter?+

A Letter of Authorisation (also called a Guarantee Letter or Surat Jaminan) is the document an insurer issues to confirm it will pay the hospital directly for a covered admission. Without it, the hospital will ask for a cash deposit — sometimes several times the estimated bill. Getting the LoA issued before or at admission is the single most important step in a cashless hospitalisation. The hospital's insurance desk handles this process, but it helps to call the insurer's 24-hour authorisation line first if you are planning a non-emergency admission.

Which insurer has the fastest LoA process in Indonesia?+

Among major insurers in Indonesia, Allianz is frequently cited for fast LoA issuance — industry data suggests under 20 minutes on average for straightforward admissions. AIA and Manulife also have dedicated 24-hour authorisation lines. Processing time varies by complexity of the case and time of day. When evaluating group health plans, ask each insurer for their average LoA turnaround time as part of your comparison.
