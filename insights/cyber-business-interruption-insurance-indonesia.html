Cyber business interruption insurance: the coverage gap Indonesian SMEs must close

By the Insurewise team · 8 June 2026 · 8 min read

Most Indonesian SME owners with a business interruption policy believe they are protected if their operations are forced to stop. They are right — as long as the cause is a fire, flood, or other physical disaster. But in 2026, the threat that is most likely to shut down a business for days or weeks is not physical at all. It is a ransomware attack, a data breach, or a system failure. And standard business interruption insurance does not cover any of them.

Cyber-Business Interruption (CBI) insurance fills this gap. It is a specialised coverage — either a standalone product or a built-in component of a broader cyber policy — that compensates a business for the income lost and the costs incurred while systems are down following a cyber event. For Indonesian SMEs operating in the Coretax era of 2026, where digital compliance is now mandatory, the stakes of even a brief system outage have never been higher.

The critical coverage gap: why your existing policy won't pay

This is the most expensive misunderstanding in Indonesian SME insurance. Standard commercial property and business interruption policies are built around one requirement: there must be direct physical loss or damage to a physical asset caused by a covered peril such as fire or flood. A cyber attack that encrypts your servers, locks your POS system, or takes your website offline causes no physical damage whatsoever. The asset — your server, your laptop, your network — is physically intact. The policy therefore has no obligation to pay.

❌ Standard BI — does NOT cover

  • Ransomware shutting down operations
  • Data breach forcing a system offline
  • Hacking that freezes digital systems
  • Cloud provider outage affecting your business
  • Lost revenue during IT recovery

✅ Cyber-BI — covers all of the above, plus

  • Lost net profit during the outage
  • Rent, payroll and fixed costs that continue
  • Digital forensics and IT recovery experts
  • Software and data restoration costs
  • Crisis communication and PR costs

⚠️ The cyber trap in plain language: if you rely on your standard BI policy to protect you from a digital shutdown, you will discover the gap at the worst possible moment — when systems are down, revenue has stopped, and your insurer tells you the claim is excluded.

What Cyber-BI insurance actually covers

While the base cyber policy covers data recovery, breach notification and third-party liability, the Business Interruption component specifically addresses the time-loss costs — often the most financially damaging for an SME:

💸 Lost net profit

Compensates the revenue the business would have earned if digital systems had remained operational — calculated against your pre-incident trading record.

🏢 Continuing fixed costs

Covers rent, staff salaries and other obligations that do not stop just because your systems do. This prevents a short outage from cascading into a payroll or lease default.

🔍 Digital forensics & recovery

Pays the high fees of IT forensics specialists and cyber incident responders — the 'digital doctors' who identify the breach, contain the damage, and restore systems. These fees run into hundreds of millions of rupiah for a serious incident.

💾 System & data restoration

Covers the cost to rebuild, restore or replace software, databases and data that were corrupted, encrypted, or destroyed by the attack.

The 2026 urgency: Coretax and the digital compliance trap

Indonesia's Coretax system — the DJP's new unified digital tax platform replacing legacy tools like e-Faktur and e-SPT — became mandatory for all taxpayers in 2026. Every Indonesian business must now file its annual tax return, invoices and payments exclusively through Coretax. This is a significant step forward for tax administration, but it creates a new category of risk for SMEs: digital compliance dependency.

📋 Real scenario: ransomware meets Coretax

The situation: A medium-sized retail chain suffers a ransomware attack that encrypts their accounting systems, POS network, and digital filing environment.

The double squeeze: With systems frozen, they cannot process sales — revenue stops immediately. Simultaneously, because everything runs through Coretax, they cannot issue tax invoices (e-Faktur) or meet filing deadlines. They face both an operational shutdown and a compliance failure at the same time, with penalties and reputational damage accruing by the day.

Without CBI coverage: The business funds its own recovery — paying IT forensics specialists out of pocket, covering two weeks of payroll with zero revenue, and absorbing late-filing penalties, all simultaneously.

With CBI coverage: The insurer's incident response team mobilises immediately. The policy funds the IT recovery specialists, replaces the lost revenue during the two-week outage, and covers the fixed costs that keep the business from defaulting on its obligations while systems are restored.

The Coretax dependency changes the risk calculus. A cyber incident is no longer just an IT problem — it is simultaneously an operational, financial, and regulatory problem. CBI insurance is the only product designed to address all three at once.

Why SMEs are the primary target — and most at risk

A common misconception is that cyber-business interruption is a concern only for large corporations with complex IT systems. In practice, SMEs are disproportionately targeted precisely because their defences are weaker. Attackers automate their campaigns, scanning for vulnerabilities at scale — and SMEs without dedicated IT security teams represent easy, frequent targets.

The consequences of a successful attack fall harder on SMEs than on large companies for a structural reason: cash reserves. A large corporation can self-fund a week of downtime. Most SMEs cannot. When revenue stops for two weeks and fixed costs continue, many businesses find they are in a liquidity crisis before the systems are even back online. Industry data consistently shows that a significant proportion of SMEs that suffer a major cyber incident do not fully recover — not because the technical damage was irreparable, but because the financial gap during recovery was too wide to bridge without outside support.

We recommend SMEs view Cyber-BI not as an 'IT policy' but as a business continuity tool — the financial bridge that keeps the company solvent during the recovery window. A digital disaster should be survivable. Without CBI coverage, for many SMEs it is not.

Frequently asked questions

Does my existing business interruption policy cover cyber attacks?+

Almost certainly not. Standard BI policies require physical loss or damage to trigger. A cyber attack — even one that completely shuts down your operations — causes no physical damage, so the standard policy excludes it. You need a cyber policy with a built-in BI component, or a standalone Cyber-BI extension.

What is the waiting period (deductible period) on a Cyber-BI policy?+

Most Cyber-BI policies include a waiting period — typically 8 to 24 hours — before the BI coverage kicks in. This is the cyber equivalent of the time excess on a standard BI policy. Shorter waiting periods cost more in premium but provide cover for brief but costly outages.

Does Cyber-BI cover a cloud provider going down?+

Some policies include 'contingent cyber BI' cover for outages caused by third-party service providers such as cloud hosts. This is policy-specific and usually subject to sub-limits. If your business is heavily cloud-dependent, confirm this with your broker — it is a key difference between products.

Is Cyber-BI only for large businesses?+

No — and SMEs arguably need it more. Larger companies can often self-fund short outages from reserves. SMEs with tighter cash positions face immediate liquidity pressure the moment revenue stops. CBI cover is available for businesses of all sizes, and for SMEs it is often the most important financial safety net in the policy.

This article is general information, not legal or insurance advice. Cyber policy terms, coverage triggers and exclusions vary significantly between insurers. Always read the policy wording carefully and confirm coverage with a licensed broker before purchasing. Coretax regulations are subject to ongoing DJP guidance.
