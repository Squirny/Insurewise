Employee Benefits

What is employee benefit, and why companies should take it

By the Insurewise team · 2 June 2026 · 6 min read

Attracting good people is hard. Keeping them is harder. In a competitive Indonesian job market, salary alone rarely wins — what increasingly tips the balance is the protection and peace of mind a company offers its team. That is exactly what an employee benefit programme delivers. This guide explains what employee benefit means, what it typically covers, how it differs from BPJS, and why offering it is one of the smartest investments a growing business can make.

What is employee benefit?

Employee benefit is the package of non-cash compensation and protection a company provides to its staff on top of their base salary, in line with their employment agreement. It goes beyond monthly pay, bonuses and holiday allowances. In practice, the core of most employee benefit programmes is group insurance: health, life and personal accident cover arranged by the employer for the whole team.

In short: employee benefit turns "we pay a salary" into "we look after you." That shift is what employees remember — and what competitors find hard to match.

What does employee benefit insurance cover?

A typical group programme is built from a few core components, which you can mix and tier by job level:

  • Group health (inpatient and outpatient) — hospitalisation, surgery, doctor visits, prescriptions and diagnostics, usually cashless at partner hospitals.
  • Group term life — a lump sum paid to an employee's family if they pass away.
  • Group personal accident — cover for death or disability caused by accidents.
  • Optional add-ons — maternity, dental, optical and outpatient riders for richer plans.

Is employee benefit the same as BPJS?

No. In Indonesia, employers are legally required to enrol staff in BPJS Kesehatan and BPJS Ketenagakerjaan — that is the mandatory foundation. Private employee benefit insurance is built on top of it, offering higher limits, faster access, private rooms, wider hospital networks and benefits BPJS does not cover. Most companies use the two together: BPJS as the base, private group cover as the upgrade employees actually feel.

Why companies should offer employee benefit

Even smaller companies gain a real, measurable edge from a well-designed programme. The benefits fall into three groups.

1. Attract and retain talent

Strong benefits make a company more appealing to high-quality candidates and signal that it values its people. Employees who feel cared for are far less likely to leave for a competitor — lowering turnover and the heavy cost of rehiring and retraining.

2. Higher productivity and wellbeing

Staff who know they and their families are protected against illness or accident worry less and focus more. Quick access to care means less time lost to untreated health issues, and a healthier, more motivated team performs better day to day.

3. Financial stability and a stronger reputation

Insurance absorbs the medical and compensation costs of a sick or injured employee, turning unpredictable expenses into a planned premium. Premiums are generally a deductible business expense, and a company known for looking after its people earns trust with employees, clients and partners alike.

How much does it cost?

Group premiums are priced per person and depend mainly on the age profile of your team and the plan tier you choose (for example, the daily hospital room limit). Because pricing is per head, costs scale naturally with your headcount — and a broker can compare insurers to find the best value for your budget.
